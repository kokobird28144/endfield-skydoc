0. Why Automatic Production Balancing? ​

At the beginning of version 1.4, gas industry brought us two great lords:

<（°0°）>Lord Pyrrolite** and Lord Heavy Xiranite\（°0°）/**

My goodness, Lord Pyrrolite sells for 70 Stock Bills! Its extraordinarily complicated processing is mind-blowing!

[Visual: a mysterious passage of long, difficult sentences demonstrating the complexity of Pyrrolite processing]

After all sorts of video creators worked their mathematical magic,

we now know that maximum production capacity means making lots of Lord Pyrrolite and Lord Heavy Xiranite, plus two dignitaries from the previous dynasty: Lord SC Wuling Battery and Yazhen Syringe [C]—er, the lowly servant.

Ideally, not a scrap of Cuprium or Xiranite should remain, and the same goes for Originium Ore and Ferrium.

To achieve this, creators first proposed a balanced combination of 14.4 Pyrrolite Parts and 24 Heavy Xiranite, then an even more extreme combination of 14.2 Pyrrolite Parts and 26 Heavy Xiranite. The latter is already very, very close to the theoretical solution!

Uh, but I am just an ordinary player. Rather than squeeze out every last bit of production, I want to ask:

[Spoken]

What if I need medicine?

What if I have not unlocked all the mines?

What if I do not want to connect a Purification Node?

What if I have not unlocked over-capacity transfer in the Valley?

[Visual]

What if all the inputs are balanced away and nothing is left to make the Yazhen Syringe [A] I need myself?

What if my mines are not all unlocked and cannot supply the line?

What if I do not want to connect a Purification Node—someone else can lay that blasted conduit—but maximum production requires it?

My Valley setup is not maxed out; I have no over-capacity transfer...

Hey, an ordinary player with all these demands? Why not use a lower-output line that leaves more inputs over?

So what? I want to use all my inputs, get high output, and satisfy all these requirements. Is that not allowed?

And so, through our efforts, automatically balanced production lines were born.

Why must “Xiranite satisfy the Pyrrolite line first, and Cuprium satisfy the Heavy Xiranite line first”? Let us look at them one by one.

The result is similar: because the Heavy Xiranite line lacks Cuprium, we have not used up the extra Xiranite.

This is very similar to the Pyrrolite line lacking Xiranite. To use the extra Xiranite, we must first satisfy the Heavy Xiranite line with Cuprium, ensuring it has enough Cuprium.

[Visual: Cuprium, Xiranite, Originium Ore, Ferrium → Pyrrolite, Heavy Xiranite, SC batteries, syringes]

Must a terminal be a single product? Of course not!

For example, in the 1.5 automatic maximum-gross-output setup, while Hetonite storage is not full, Hetonite and Pyrrolite output stays at 1:2. The Cuprium terminal is then not simply Pyrrolite, but “Hetonite and Pyrrolite at 1:2”.

We can regard a terminal combining several products as the product of a custom recipe. It then works just like the single-product terminals above.

Introducing combined products as terminals gives us more kinds of products! Perhaps at the end of this road lies the ultimate all-item automatic balancing...

5. Recap ​

[Visual: Cuprium, Xiranite, Originium Ore, Ferrium → Pyrrolite, Heavy Xiranite, SC batteries, syringes] (Or fade it into the background?)

Let us organize what we have covered:

For the whole automatic-balancing system, remember just two points: (consider letting these two lines fill the screen)

  1. Each input has a corresponding terminal: Cuprium corresponds to Pyrrolite, which can consume all the Cuprium, and Xiranite corresponds to Heavy Xiranite, which can consume all the Xiranite. A terminal may also combine products, such as “Hetonite and Pyrrolite at 1:2” serving as the Cuprium terminal in the preceding section.

  2. Each input first satisfies the other lines using that input; only the remainder goes to its terminal. For example, Cuprium first satisfies the Heavy Xiranite line, ensuring that it can consume all the Xiranite. The remaining Cuprium then goes to the Pyrrolite line, the Cuprium terminal. In the five Chinese characters “先供非末端”: supply non-terminals first. We will repeatedly use those five characters in future explanations of automatic balancing.

# An explanation of Separator Cores for the description:
From this video, we can say that neither Pyrrolite nor Heavy Xiranite can consume all its Cuprium/Xiranite if another input is short. Treat Separator Cores as a special input, then: naturally, sufficient Separator Cores are needed for Pyrrolite/Heavy Xiranite to consume all the Cuprium/Xiranite. We need not think about what Separator Cores are made from—or do we?
With the current recipes, we can indeed treat it that way. But readers willing to come to the description probably want more than this rough explanation, so let me modify the Heavy Xiranite recipe slightly to make the statement fail:
(Original recipe) Xiranite + Separator Core → (modified recipe) Cuprium + Separator Core.
If we now freely supply Heavy Xiranite production with Separator Cores, it competes directly for them with the Pyrrolite line. If Heavy Xiranite takes too many, Pyrrolite becomes constrained by the reduced Separator Core supply and cannot consume the Cuprium left over by other lines. (Interestingly, taking too few Separator Cores means Pyrrolite receives enough. In other words, satisfy Pyrrolite's Separator Cores first, then let Heavy Xiranite consume the remainder. Heavy Xiranite becomes the Separator Core terminal, and Separator Cores become the Xiranite terminal—equivalent to Heavy Xiranite correctly serving as the Xiranite terminal.) Fundamentally, Heavy Xiranite has not correctly served as the Xiranite terminal here. One correct approach is therefore to supply the Heavy Xiranite line with Xiranite left over by other lines and sufficient Cuprium, rather than with Separator Cores, incorporating production of its Separator Cores into the Heavy Xiranite line itself.
In any case, remember that automatic balancing works because terminal lines correctly fulfill their terminal roles. This conclusion broadly follows the mathematics. Keeping Separator Cores sufficiently supplied or coupling their production into a terminal line are merely ways to make an actual line obey “supply non-terminals first” in different situations. (Yes, the solution in my example is not the only option.)
If you have read this far, you probably understand why the video did not explain Separator Cores as an intermediate—it does get dizzying. Yet they cannot be avoided in concrete line design. We put this text in the description in the hope it helps you understand/design automatic balancing yourself (heart hands).
