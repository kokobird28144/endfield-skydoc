JNK's Introductory Article on Blocking Flow

The above article includes a method of constructing splitting through blocking to implement arbitrary rational-rate limiters, and a flow-calculation algorithm that has been shown not always to be correct.

madSUN's rules (port flow variables, close to the implementation)

  1. Flow conservation

  2. Assign each edge four variables v_H_in, v_L_in, v_H_out, v_L_out (H = High, L = Low, describing the high/low flow relationships under blocking and flowing conditions) and one binary variable is_blocked indicating whether it is blocked.
    2.1. If is_blocked == 1 (blocked), v = v_H_in = v_L_out; if is_blocked == 0 (not blocked), v = v_L_in = v_H_out. Also require v_H_* > v_L_*. (This rule means blocking and non-blocking are exclusive: it is either blocked or not, and the high flow is always greater than the low flow.)
    2.2. If the upstream component is a Splitter, its downstream edges share v_H_in; if the downstream component is a Converger, its upstream edges share v_H_out. (This corresponds to equal flows among a Splitter's non-blocked downstream edges and a Converger's blocked upstream edges.)

  3. Outputs are not blocked.

恒星泰斗's rules (whether ports are serviced round-robin)

Round-robin ports have the maximum flow rate.

Round-robin ports have equal flow rates.

Total inflow and outflow at each node are equal.

Full rank (the flow relationships admit no multiple solutions).

Cipolla_'s rules (blocking/flowing and full belts)

  1. A Splitter's upstream is flowing <=> at least one downstream edge is flowing.

  2. A Splitter's upstream is blocked <=> all downstream edges are blocked (invalid when the upstream belt is full).

  3. A Converger's downstream is blocked ＜=＞ at least one of its upstream edges is blocked.

  4. A Converger's downstream is flowing <=> all upstream edges are flowing (invalid when the downstream belt is full).

  5. When a Splitter has a flowing downstream branch, all branches with the highest flow rate are flowing; all other branches are not flowing.

  6. When a Converger has a blocked upstream branch, all branches with the highest flow rate are blocked; all other branches are not blocked.

  7. Every belt must be flowing or blocked, and may be both. A full belt must be both flowing and blocked.

  8. Flow conservation.

  9. All system inputs are blocked, and all outputs are flowing.

jnk's rules (three states of blocking/flowing and full belts)

0．Every belt must occupy one of three states: non-blocked, both flowing and blocked, or non-flowing.
1．If a Splitter's input is flowing, at least one output is flowing; if every output is non-flowing, its input is non-flowing.
2．If a Splitter's input is non-flowing, at least one output is non-flowing.
3．If a Splitter's input is non-blocked, at least one output is non-blocked.
4．All flowing outputs of a Splitter have equal flow rates; non-flowing outputs do not exceed that rate.
5．If a Splitter's input is non-flowing and a flowing output exists, that input's flow rate is 1.
6．If a Converger's output is non-flowing, at least one input is non-flowing.
7．If a Converger's output is non-blocked, all inputs are non-blocked.
8．All blocked inputs of a Converger have equal flow rates; non-blocked inputs do not exceed that rate.
9．If a Converger's input is non-flowing and its output is flowing, that input's flow rate is 1.
10．Flow conservation; all system outputs are non-blocked. Source output edges have equal flow rates and, when non-blocked, must carry real flow.
(Linear-programming solution rule) Every flowing edge has flow at least the global minimum; the objective is to maximize that minimum.
