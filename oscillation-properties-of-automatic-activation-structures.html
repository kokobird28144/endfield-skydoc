5.2 Analyzing Oscillations in an Automatic Activation Setup ​

Source page ID: mi7Oi0CH9sH6cuWCBGpkNt | Open in Tencent Docs

Oscillation analysis operates on an intermediate timescale: slower than the fast timescale of individual discrete items, but faster than the slow timescale of long-term averages. Flow-rate models remain usable, but must account for changes in flow between stages caused by blocking.

Let us now discuss more examples of blocking-feedback oscillation, beginning with automatic activation/adaptive setups for Fluid-Gas Transmuting Units and similar facilities.

Some Simpler Adaptive Setups

Video introduction to this page:

Arknights: Endfield / AIC Oscillation Studies: How to Overwhelm an Adaptive Xiragen Fluid-Gas Unit (Warning: Contains Equations)

The model uses several approximations and is not guaranteed to match the discrete case exactly, though its overall conclusions should be reasonably sound. Buffer problems tend to occur most readily in setups such as four-core Pyrrolite oscillation, where 1 Acridgen consumes 40 Hetonite Gas. Here we first pick an easy target—cross that out—a representative example to demonstrate the analysis. Work on other setups and coupling between modules is ongoing…

Model Overview and Variables ​

First consider the simple setup below. A multi-port conduit sends Liquid Xiranite to a Fluid-Gas Transmuting Unit. Once the unit is full, fluid overflows to the activation port, triggering Xiragen production that consumes Liquid Xiranite. Overflow then stops and Liquid Xiranite continues flowing into the unit, forming a cycle. Analyzing this simple setup quickly reveals how many of the oscillation issues of interest arise.

Let us first collect the conditions already known:

  1. This setup can clearly handle at most 36 Liquid Xiranite input (per minute; omitted below): 30 for Xiragen production and 6 for activation. With input below 36, the overflow design keeps sufficient Liquid Xiranite inside the machine. It also sends <6 Liquid Xiranite to activation, so the machine is not continuously activated and activation fluid is not wasted.

  2. This gives two rules for modeling: the Fluid-Gas Transmuting Unit's Liquid Xiranite buffer is always above zero; and each 1 Liquid Xiranite entering the machine enables 10 seconds of operation, during which 1 Liquid Xiranite is consumed to produce 1 Xiragen every 2 seconds.

  3. Furthermore, if input exceeds 30, the machine buffer can remain full because the unit's maximum processing rate is only 30. Oscillation then appears only as changes in the rate entering the overflow pipe: faster while the machine is stopped, slower while it runs.

  4. There is also the case of input below 6. The overflow pipe then remains empty, and only the machine buffer oscillates.

To analyze oscillation, separate the buffers in different parts of the setup and examine how each changes in each stage. Begin with the simpler assumption that the Liquid Xiranite input rate into the conduit is a constant v0<36. As shown, let Liquid Xiranite inventory in the conduit be s0, the combined Liquid Xiranite inventory in the machine and its connecting pipes be s1, and pipe inventory between the overflow branch and the rate-6 limiter be s2. Liquid Xiranite flows from the conduit toward the machine at v1 and fills s2 through overflow at v2. Also note the full pipe length L from the overflow outlet to the activation port. It determines how long after overflow the transmuting unit activates; L is 8 tiles in the example above.

Think about it: why can we ignore Liquid Xiranite's transit time through the lower pipe, but must account for transit through the upper overflow pipe?

Stage-by-Stage Analysis of Each Case ​

Input Between 6 and 30 per Minute ​

Now write the governing equations for each stage. First consider v0<30, starting with empty conduit and overflow-pipe buffers and a stopped machine. This is simply the buffer-filling stage:

s1(t+dt)=s1(t)+v0∗dt

When does it fill? Assume a minimum buffer level S_low and an upper capacity C1. Together with v0, these directly determine this stage's duration. We will return to determine s_low at the end.

Tfill=(C1−Slow)/v0

Next comes the overflow stage. Before Liquid Xiranite reaches the activation port, it enters s2 at v0 and starts filling it. This continues until the machine starts, which depends on when the first unit of Liquid Xiranite arrives. The limiter does not delay this first unit; with a pipe transport speed of 2 tiles per second,

Toverflow=L/2∗(1/60)=L/120

This stage has two branches: s2 either remains below capacity or fills completely. Count the overflow pipe tiles to obtain capacity C2, then calculate its filling time Tfill−2=C2/(v0−6). Comparing the two times determines whether s2 fills. If not, its inventory is (v0−6)L/120. If it fills, inventory is C2 and the conduit accumulates an additional Soutlet=(v0−6)L/120−C2.

For v0<6, take the buffer inventory as 0.

Then comes the machine-operating stage. Its duration depends on the total amount of overflowing Liquid Xiranite. Again there are two cases: with v0<30, the conduit stops overflowing and all input replenishes the buffer consumed by the machine; with v0>30, the conduit can replenish this buffer while still overflowing a small amount.

First analyze the simpler case, s2 not filling completely, whose condition is

C2/(v0−6)>L/120⇒120C2>L(v0−6)

The operating time can be determined without using s2: all Liquid Xiranite that overflows from the beginning of the overflow stage provides operating time at 1/6 minute per unit, giving

Twork=v0∗L/120∗(1/6)=v0L/720

During this interval, s1 falls at 30-v0, so at the end of the cycle

C1−Slow=(30−v0)Twork=(30−v0)v0L/720

The total cycle length is

T=Tfill+Toverflow+Twork=(30−v0)L/720+L/120+v0L/720=L/20

Now consider s2 filling completely. The difference is that inventory in the conduit flows out at 36 to replenish both pipe buffers, providing additional Liquid Xiranite for activation. The time required to clear the conduit buffer is

Tdrain=Soutlet/(36−v0)=((v0−6)L/120−C2)/(36−v0)

After the conduit buffer clears, Liquid Xiranite no longer overflows. We can thus determine the total Liquid Xiranite used for activation and the corresponding operating time. Here, we must also include the Liquid Xiranite that passed through the limiter during the overflow stage.

Twork=Toverflow+(Tdrain∗6+C2)∗1/6=Toverflow+Tdrain+C2/6

No separate addition was needed in the unfilled case because operating time was calculated directly from the total amount of fluid; here that total is split into parts.

This is easy to interpret: Liquid Xiranite that has already passed the limiter activates the machine first; the machine then remains activated while the conduit still contains a backlog, and finally relies on the full s2 buffer after the conduit clears. The machine buffer likewise begins to decrease at 30-v0 only after the conduit buffer clears. The buffer consumption corresponding to the activation Liquid Xiranite represented by Toverflow must also be included. Therefore,

C1−Slow=(30−v0)(C2/6+L/120)

The total cycle simplifies to

T=180C2/v0−6C2+9L/v036−v0

The operating-time expression is also given:

Twork=−C2v0/6+5C2+L/436−v0
What the hell is this?

Input Above 30 per Minute ​

Now consider v0 between 30 and 36. As noted earlier, the machine buffer stays full and does not oscillate. There is no buffer-filling stage: begin immediately after the machine stops, at the overflow stage. We still have

Toverflow=L/2∗(1/60)=L/120

The overflow pipe receives fluid at v0, and again s2 may or may not fill. Here, however, the analysis simplifies: whether or not s2 fills and whether or not excess inventory accumulates in the conduit, all of this inventory can be treated as activation fluid. Unlike the earlier case, none of the conduit backlog must replenish the machine buffer: external supply v0 can provide that replenishment directly. We may therefore treat the two buffers as one, whose effect will become clear shortly.

During the machine-operating stage, v0-30 Liquid Xiranite continues entering the overflow pipe after the conduit buffer clears. The actual operating time is determined, approximately, by the time needed to clear the overflow-pipe and conduit inventories. As described above, it is

Twork=v0∗(L/120)/(36−v0)

The total cycle length is

T=3L/(10∗(36−v0))

Input Below 6 per Minute ​

Finally consider v0 below 6. The overflow-pipe inventory s2 remains zero because overflow at v0 passes straight through the limiter. There are still buffer-filling and overflow stages. This is equivalent to the v0<30 case with an unfilled overflow buffer, so the same formulas apply directly.

Avoiding Buffer Depletion ​

We may want to design the machine so that its buffer never runs dry, i.e. Slow>0. The expressions above give the following safety criterion:

min(20C2+L,v0L/6)<120C1/(30−v0)

The right-hand side has a minimum of 4C1. Since the machine buffer holds at least 50, this is at least 200. On the left, moving the limiter to the conduit outlet makes C2 very small by preventing too much Liquid Xiranite from overflowing. Even without considering C2, the left-hand side is bounded by v0L/6, whose maximum is 5L. So unless you build some monstrosity like a 40m activation pipe, the buffer cannot run dry.

The properties of min and the quadratic function also give a stricter but more general condition:

(30−v0)min(20C2+L,v0L/6)≤(30−v0)v0L/6≤37.5L

Thus, it is enough to satisfy

L<3.2C1

to guarantee the safety criterion.

Material Conservation ​

Although not used in the derivation, inspection shows that the following conservation relation holds:

Twork∗36=T∗v0

This confirms that the stage-by-stage derivation is consistent with direct material conservation over a longer timescale—simply calculating the ratio 6 Xiranite = 5 Xiragen. Our analysis reconstructs the process that was previously averaged out.

Properties of the Oscillation ​

One important finding from this line is that this blocking-feedback setup turns uniform input into oscillating output. Its period and amplitude may depend on the line's intrinsic structure (the L/20 branch), and may additionally depend on the input rate (the other branches).

What happens with oscillating input? The earlier results suggest that bursts of Liquid Xiranite supply make matters worse if their peaks occur during overflow, but have little effect if they occur during buffer filling. Input phase must therefore be considered too. Could the setup's behavior also adjust to the oscillating input—for example, a peak during overflow delays the next filling stage, eventually forcing a signal with the same frequency? This requires further research.

What About Copper-Gas Production…? ​

A modest extension of the analysis can also represent copper-gas production and multi-core Pyrrolite Gas production. They resemble the present setup in several ways: one overflowing unit of fluid enables 10 seconds of operation, buffer supply has priority, and so on. One main difference is the input-consumption rate while running. Replace 30 with 30K: K=2 represents single-machine copper-gas production, while K=8 represents four-core Pyrrolite production with respect to its Hetonite Gas input. Another difference is the response-time calculation. With overflow-based production from Some Simpler Adaptive Setups, Liquid Xiranite overflow is fixed at half the full rate. To determine when overflow stops, start from activation, when the blocked copper must first be consumed. External Liquid Xiranite then stops entering the conduit, but the fluid remaining inside continues overflowing for activation. The response time is consequently longer. Together, these differences explain why adaptive copper production often has long-period, large-amplitude oscillations and a risk of running its buffers dry. The detailed analysis will follow another time.
