An SC Wuling Battery supplies 3200W for 40s. A Depot Unloader outputs at 0.5 items/s, so a full belt supplies energy at 32000.540=64000. Since facility power consumption is a multiple of 5, write total consumption as 5x+200 (200 is the built-in generation). The generation fraction we need is therefore

Damping Theory ​

Theoretical reference: Damping Theory, Revisions, and Extensions (Output Priority).

Often used for overflow devices; both water pipes and Transport Belts follow this theory.

Calculation example:

Call the upper-right Stash A and lower-right Stash B. The upper-right Stash is placed first, giving Stash order AB according to the theory. The logistics components at the left Stash's three outputs are placed top, middle, bottom.

First calculate all possible damping values for A. (The theory also adds 1 for a factory input, but since no paths are open-ended here, ignore that.) The Belt Bridge's paths have damping 7 and 10 (two paths); the splitter's path has damping 3; the converger's path has damping 7.

All three logistics-path inlets on the left have been counted, so no recalculation for B is needed.

Take the minimum for the same path inlet, then apply the shared maximum damping for paths not starting at a converger. Damping from top to bottom is therefore 7,7,7.

Because the Belt Bridge was placed first, it has priority, so the Belt Bridge and splitter output round-robin. If the converger were placed first, it would output with priority.
