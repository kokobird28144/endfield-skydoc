Full-Storage Overflow Compatible with 1.5 Automatic Gross Production ​

Source page ID: nhFNnz7suUdKtA9LLLBMc0 | Open in Tencent Docs

Full-storage overflow has a small update for 1.6. Before updating, do not route Xiragen from the new map back here, or you risk running short of Separator Cores.

Preface ​

Our 1.5 automatic gross-production setup used the brute-force technique of empty-storage balancing. As the number of samples grew rapidly, however, we found that unloaders providing oversupply could fail to unload for extended periods. This “loss of oversupply” blocks lines such as Xircon.

The Development Bureau therefore offers two approaches: full-storage oscillation and full-storage overflow.

This Article Explains Full-Storage Overflow and Provides Installation Blueprints ​

Author: BEAR (credits at the end). This article primarily exchanges design ideas; blueprint codes and ease of copying still need optimization.

Core Idea ​

First answer two questions: what is full-storage overflow? How does it guarantee ample Xircon/Separator Core inputs while avoiding the oversupply required by empty-storage balancing, and thus the possibility of losing that oversupply?

Full-storage overflow means applying overflow routing where solid Xiranite/Cuprium is originally produced. These two inputs for Xircon/Separator Core enter depot storage first; surplus Xiranite/Cuprium becomes Heavy Xiranite/Cuprium Powder locally.

Solid Xiranite/Cuprium has priority for storage, while Heavy Xiranite/Cuprium Powder inputs are not unloaded from storage. Xircon/Separator Core therefore receives ample inputs with priority.

That is: overflow provides supply priority; full storage ensures sufficient supply.

Empty-storage balancing, full-storage oscillation and full-storage overflow have no absolute ranking. Here are the latter's advantages and disadvantages, so you can choose for your needs:

Disadvantages ​

1. Full-storage overflow requires one Forge, one crucible and one transmuting unit per module, because we assume uneven depositing into storage—one disadvantage arising from this project's advantages. The footprint is enormous: Jingyu Valley + Marker Stone fit only dual SC Wuling Battery production + 12 Xiranite units + single-core Cuprium Canister + 4 Thermal Banks, with no possibility of all-item compatibility.

2. The many facilities consume substantial power: 5 pumps in Jingyu Valley and 10 in Marker Stone.

3. Heavy Xiranite rates are hard to measure: fluctuations are so large that even a 2h report barely becomes a straight line.

4. (I have not thought of another?)

Advantages ​

1. The Xiranite module has a broad 0–360 oscillation range and an 8+4 modular structure. Players with incomplete technology and 4 or 8 Forges are supported, provided Xiranite output covers Xircon/Separator Core consumption.

2. It ignores all depositing round-robin/priority phenomena. Every Forge has its own overflow component; no loader misbehavior affects the structure.

3. Additional modules can be added freely provided total Xiranite draw does not exceed 360 and Cuprium draw does not exceed 240. (The 240 ceiling could be raised, but I am lazy.) For Chubby Lung, choose any available module you like and rate-limit it if it draws too much. No specially designed oversupply line is needed. The supplied Xircon/Separator Core lines can likewise be replaced with whichever you prefer.

4. After Cuprium-series and battery storage fill, Heavy Xiranite runs at full rate. All three outposts can then sell only Heavy Xiranite and still meet voucher requirements.

5. No cross-region conduits are mandatory except those for gas deposits in the North Wuling Exclusion Zone.
