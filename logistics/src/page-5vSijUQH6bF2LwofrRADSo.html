3. Easily Misread Requirements ​

  • “Does not contain Sugar” differs from “not sweet.” The former checks ingredients; the latter checks traits. A dessert does not necessarily have the sweet trait.

  • “Vegetarian” differs from “does not contain Meat.” Meat-based and seafood are also independent traits; ingredient names alone cannot substitute for them.

  • “Might I have a drink that is not spicy?” actually checks for no alcohol aroma. Bottled Milk and Bowl of Tea are among the options. Do not filter beverages using the spicy trait for food.

  • “Hey, do you have any Relishroom soup/dishes?” requires Relishroom [A]. Neither spoken line stresses the premium quality, but the distinction still matters when using the table.

  • “Might I have something sweet that is not a pastry?” requires sweet and excludes dessert. Eight Treasure Rice and Berry-Sweet Sip are among the options.

  • Shredded Tuber & Pepper does not automatically satisfy spicy just because its name mentions pepper. The check uses meal traits, not real-world taste.

  • Normal satisfaction is not perfect satisfaction. For example, “A soup, something light” requires soup, not spicy, and not salty. Some meals that are not soup still meet part of the request and therefore appear in the normal-satisfaction column. Do not remove these partial matches based on the dish name or category, or treat them as perfect choices.

  • Assign meals separately to each request. The same serving cannot occupy two independent request slots. One meal may suit several requests, but you still need enough servings.
