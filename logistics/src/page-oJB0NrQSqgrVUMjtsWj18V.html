Sewage Accumulation and Inlet Recirculation Rate Limiting in an Adaptive Xircon Module ​

Compiled on 2026-10-15. This is a research record of the present discussion and experimental feedback, not a reproduction of an original Tencent Docs page.

Current progress: the user reports that the inlet recirculation/rate-limiting scheme passed two simulator conditions: B and C each at the full 30/min, and each limited to 24/min. The modified scheme has not yet been tested in-game. Theoretical balancing values, conditional timing models, and simulator results are presented separately and do not substitute for one another.

Purpose of the Setup ​

Use 3 Expanded Crucibles, 1 ordinary Reactor Crucible, and 1 Purification Unit to build a module that reduces external Liquid Xiranite supply as Xircon demand changes.

At full load, the target is to supply 30/min Liquid Xiranite externally and produce 75/min Xircon. When blocked Xircon output slows production, the intention is to reduce external Liquid Xiranite while keeping each of the three Expanded Crucibles at full liquefaction rate:

text
30/min Xiranite + 30/min Clean Water → 30/min Liquid Xiranite

For example, reducing Xircon output from 75/min to 73.75/min should reduce external Liquid Xiranite from 30/min to 28/min.

This discussion focuses on resolving persistent Sewage accumulation during slowdown and examining whether the inlet structure can reduce dependence on reaction phase. One or more crucibles may absorb the Xircon output blockage; the specific arrangement considered here keeps A fixed and slows B and C equally.

“Adaptive” here has a limited output range. With internal Liquid Xiranite production fixed at 90/min, the module cannot continue indefinitely while Xircon production remains completely stopped. Buffering and shutdown after a complete production stop have not been resolved in this discussion.

Overall Balance ​

Let Xircon output be Q, the total liquid-reaction rate across the four crucibles be R, and external Liquid Xiranite supply be E, all per minute.

If all inert effluent is purified and recycled, no material is lost, and inventories show no continuing trend, each liquid reaction provides 1 effluent directly and recovers a further 1/4 through purification:

text
2Q = R + R/4
R = 1.6Q
E = R - 90 = 1.6Q - 90

Thus, 75/min Xircon requires 120/min liquid reactions: all four crucibles must achieve 30/min, exactly exhausting this recipe's capacity. If timing persistently prevents one crucible from performing enough liquid reactions, the others have no extra capacity to compensate.

External supply cannot be negative. Material conservation alone therefore requires Q ≥ 56.25/min when internal liquefaction is fixed at 90/min. The 56.25–75/min interval is a theoretical candidate range, not a tested operating range for the inlet recirculation structure.

Experiment 1: Sewage Accumulation After Xircon Slowdown ​

Original Observation Conditions ​

The early in-game observations concerned a crucible before the inlet recirculation modification:

  • Xircon inventory reached 50, with output limited to 24/min: 4 items every 10 s, followed by a pause.
  • No external Sewage entered; external effluent was plentiful.
  • Liquefaction consistently produced 1 Liquid Xiranite every 2 s, with no observed blockage of that reaction.
  • Liquid Xiranite inventory stayed empty; any material that could not react was exported directly.
  • Inert effluent inventory stayed empty and drained normally.

After switching the crucible off and on, two kinds of behavior appeared. In one, Sewage remained unchanged during roughly 1 minute of observation. In the other, Sewage first oscillated, then gradually increased. In the latter case, the liquid reaction intermittently stopped in an “already complete, awaiting product inventory transfer” state.

The effluent inventory display was mostly between 48 and 50, but refreshed about once per second. It cannot reconstruct subsecond arrivals, input consumption, and inventory transfers. The impression that “the UI shows continuous operation” was also revised: Liquid Xiranite export was still visible.

Analytical Limits Established by the Observations ​

  1. Inputs are consumed when a reaction starts. Products that cannot enter inventory can remain in that recipe's completion buffer, preventing the next batch of the same recipe from starting.
  2. With a free buffer and Sewage present, newly generated Liquid Xiranite enters the liquid reaction. An additional fixed window during which the machine refuses new Liquid Xiranite after the buffer clears must therefore not be assumed.
  3. With the original direct feed, the first external unit can enter immediately after a space appears; the second takes approximately 0.5 s.
  4. Neither blocked inert effluent nor directly supplied external Sewage caused the observed Sewage growth in this crucible.
  5. The timing analysis assumes that completed products can transfer from their buffer whenever space is available. The exact ordering of simultaneous external input and internal transfer still requires event-by-event confirmation, not inference from refreshed inventory displays.

Mechanism Analysis: Starting, Completing, and Transferring Products Are Different Events ​

Where the Phase Problem Arises ​

Distinguish four events: Xircon leaving the outlet; the crystal reaction starting and consuming effluent; the liquid reaction reaching 100%; and buffered effluent actually entering the crucible's inventory.

A crystal-reaction start releases two effluent spaces. If the liquid reaction is already complete, its buffer can use that space. If not, it cannot reserve the space in advance. During the wait, two external effluent units may refill the crucible first.

The liquid reaction then completes but keeps waiting for space, so its next batch cannot start. Subsequently generated Liquid Xiranite leaves, while Sewage that should have been consumed remains inside.

The fact that buffer transfers depend on the crystal reaction releasing space does not synchronize the next liquid-reaction start with the crystal reaction. Without Liquid Xiranite inventory, the next start must wait for a new unit on the independent liquefaction cycle.

At the cycle boundary, a previous batch is buffered and awaiting transfer; sufficient Sewage is already present. External effluent refills spaces according to the original direct-feed behavior.

In the accumulation example, the key interval is: at 2 s the crystal reaction consumes two effluent and external input immediately adds one unit; at 2.5 s a second external unit refills the space; at 3 s the liquid reaction completes but cannot transfer its products, and newly produced Liquid Xiranite leaves at the same time; at 4 s the crystal reaction consumes inputs again, finally allowing the old buffer to empty.

What repeats here is the reaction/input timing, not the Sewage inventory in the accumulation example. Sewage grows until an inventory boundary changes the operating state. This timeline cannot be extended indefinitely as a true steady state.

Normal Surplus Versus Extra Export ​

With liquefaction at 30/min and Xircon at 24/min in one crucible, a non-trending Sewage inventory requires the liquid reaction to average 24/min as well. The crucible should therefore export 6/min Liquid Xiranite, or 1 unit every 10 s. That is normal.

For the corresponding-phase 10 s window above, with no external Sewage, no Sewage outlet, 4 actual crystal-product transfers, 5 Liquid Xiranite generated, and no Liquid Xiranite retained:

text
Net Sewage change = crystal-product inventory transfers - liquid-reaction starts
Liquid Xiranite exported = 5 - liquid-reaction starts
Liquid Xiranite exported = 1 + net Sewage change

Observe extra export above the normal surplus, rather than diagnosing accumulation whenever Liquid Xiranite leaves. General observation windows must use actual product transfers; crystal-reaction starts are not a direct substitute.

The Proposed 0.25 s Recovery Time and Full-Rate Output ​

An additional 0.25 s recovery time after blocking was proposed during the discussion. However, event-by-event data did not fully establish which events it separates, or whether it is the same for different reactions. Later confirmation that fresh Liquid Xiranite can react immediately when its buffer is free led to withdrawal of the explanation based on a “0.25 s refusal-to-react window.”

A zero-recovery-delay model can already produce competition for space, so additional recovery time is not necessary for this mechanism.

Likewise, full-rate crystal production at 30/min guarantees only an input-consumption opportunity every 2 s; it does not automatically guarantee internal liquid-product transfers. Under conditional examples with unrestricted external refilling, one can still construct a timeline where the liquid reaction starts only every 4 s and Sewage keeps increasing. This is a model counterexample, not a new in-game measurement at full output.

Constraints on Modifying the Arrangement ​

Removing External Effluent Lowers Reachable Output ​

Retain the baseline layout in which the small crucible only produces effluent. If all three Expanded Crucibles use only their own effluent, each has at most 30/min liquid reactions, supporting at most 15/min Xircon: only 45/min combined. External effluent cannot be removed entirely; input timing or the feedback structure must be addressed.

Retaining Liquid Xiranite Inventory Conflicts with Continuous Full-Rate Liquefaction ​

Opening a Liquid Xiranite outlet helps send surplus to the small crucible, but leaves no internal supply buffer. Closing it permits a reserve, yet after a slowdown the reserve may fill and eventually stop liquefaction.

Adding finite storage only buffers transients; it does not resolve a long-term imbalance. Inventory-threshold control is another design, and cannot be treated as solved by merely saying “add a buffer.”

No Additional Sewage Outlet Is Available ​

B and C already use their two outlets for inert effluent and surplus Liquid Xiranite. Since each outlet can be assigned only one liquid, Sewage cannot simply be drained while retaining both existing functions.

Slowing Only A Also Exceeds the Small Crucible's Capacity ​

Keep B and C at 30/min each, and let A produce a/min Xircon. A receives no external effluent and exports none of its own, so its steady-state liquid reaction must run at 2a/min. The small crucible then requires:

text
1.6(60 + a) - (60 + 2a) = 36 - 0.4a

At a = 15, the small crucible needs exactly 30/min. At a < 15, it needs more than 30/min. Under this assignment, A therefore cannot take the slowdown alone.

These constraints apply to the specified equipment roles and direct connections; they do not prove impossibility for all mixed-fluid pipes, feedback, and timing-control structures.

The final splitter sits directly against the crucible inlet, with its other branch returning to the converger. The exact priority-reducing port, other branch destinations, loop length, and initial inventory have not been supplied in a construction drawing; this record does not invent those details.

Keeping the final component against the inlet reduces the pipe segment that can store liquid after splitting, preventing a modified upstream sequence from accumulating before the crucible and being released in a burst on recovery. That proximity does not itself prove uniform input.

The Actual 60/min Waveform ​

The user confirmed that this limiter delivers for 5 s and pauses for 5 s, rather than delivering one unit uniformly every second.

At an average 60/min with delivery during half the time, active-phase delivery is one unit every 0.5 s. If the final splitter alternates between two open branches, a conditional sequence is:

text
Time (s)        0      0.5     1      1.5     2      2.5     3      3.5     4      4.5
Destination     inlet  return  inlet  return  inlet  return  inlet  return  inlet  return
Next interval   pause for 5 s, then begin the next cycle

Under this ideal split, inlet supply averages 30/min, but adjacent arrivals during delivery are only 1 s apart. Blocking can change how the splitter redirects material into recirculation and distributes it after recovery, so the actual inlet sequence requires separate recording.

This structure must not be interpreted as “strictly one unit every 2 s.” A strict-interval model was previously used to argue for free-space protection, but the actual structure differs; their claimed equivalence must be withdrawn.

Current Mechanistic Explanation ​

For the strict-interval model, an already running liquid reaction has at most 2 s remaining. If external liquid can occupy at most one of the two spaces freed by the crystal reaction during that interval, the internal liquid product has an opportunity to enter inventory. This is the free-space protection approach, dependent on the assumed completion-buffer transfer rule.

For the actual 5 s delivery/5 s pause recirculation structure, the delivery phase may still preempt free space, while the pause may allow internal liquid products to enter inventory and compensate for earlier under-consumption of Sewage. Recirculation, priority, and the final component's proximity to the inlet jointly change the arrival sequence and may also contribute.

The latter explanation is currently a candidate mechanism. Stable simulator operation supports the structure's effectiveness under tested conditions, but cannot identify the decisive timing interval from the outcome alone or establish coverage of every initial phase.

Interim Conclusions ​

Starting from average-flow balancing, this discussion identified possible persistent Sewage accumulation under the original direct-feed inlet. Conditional timing models show that external effluent occupying space first can leave liquid-reaction products stuck in their completion buffer, reducing Sewage consumption. This mechanism does not require an extra 0.25 s recovery time and cannot be excluded merely by full-rate Xircon output.

The inlet recirculation/rate-limiting structure has user-reported simulator support: B and C each at 30/min and each at 24/min both operated stably. Its actual waveform is 5 s delivery followed by 5 s pause, so the uniform-one-arrival-every-2-s proof does not apply. It should currently be recorded as an engineering scheme supported by two simulator conditions, with its precise stability mechanism and in-game applicability awaiting validation.
